# Gentle Shore — World Lore

## The Village

Gentle Shore is a small coastal village at the end of a two-hour coastal road that most people only take on purpose. It has a proper harbor that handles modest fishing traffic, a row of cottage storefronts on the high street, and exactly the number of benches on the festival green that a reasonable person would want.

The village's permanent population is small enough that everyone knows everyone's dog's name. In the week before the Seashell Festival, that population roughly triples with seasonal visitors, returning former residents, and the occasional traveler who saw the banners from the road.

## The Seashell Festival Tradition

The festival dates to a story the oldest residents tell in at least three inconsistent versions: either a fishing family was saved from a bad storm by following a trail of luminous seashells, or the shells were laid out deliberately by a lighthouse keeper to mark the safe channel, or the whole thing started as a way to get people to clean up after a storm and the shells were just lying around.

What everyone agrees on is that the festival has been held on the same weekend every year for as long as anyone living can remember, and that stopping now would feel very strange.

**What the festival involves:**

- Shell-painting stations where visitors paint found shells (not collected ones — the festival committee has a policy) and leave them on a display rack at the harbor mouth
- A seashell-decoration competition with categories including "most ambitious," "best use of one color," and the permanent wildcard category simply called "Daffodil's Choice"
- Fresh food from local families, rotating by household each year
- A reading of the founding story — a different version is chosen by vote each year, which has caused minor but cheerful controversy for decades
- An unofficial "find the cat" tradition that started three years ago when a similar incident occurred and has shown no signs of ending

**The festival green** runs from the high street to the cliff-edge path that looks out over the harbor. Festival stalls line the high street side; the open grass in the middle is for children, dogs (on leads — Daffodil's rule), and anyone who needs to sit down. The tide-pool path splits off halfway down and winds down to the beach.

## Tone and Feel

Gentle Shore should feel like a place that takes small things seriously in a warm way, not a precious or ironic way. Problems here are real problems — a missing cat matters to Hazel the same way a missing key matters to anyone — but they are met with community and patience rather than alarm.

Nothing is darker here than it needs to be. The sea is present and respected, not threatening.
