# Verdant Aria — World Bible

## The Lost Crown

A generation ago, the kingdom of Verdant Aria possessed a crown of unusual weight —
not gold, or not only gold. The stories disagree on the metal; they agree on this:
when the crown was gone, the succession broke with it. No regent has held legitimate
claim since. The throne room has been locked by three separate factions who each
insist they are merely "preserving" it.

The crown's disappearance was not announced. It was managed — quietly, then less
quietly, then papered over with an official story no one believes. The official story
is that the crown is on "sacred loan" to a monastery in the eastern passes. No
pilgrimage to that monastery has ever confirmed this.

## The Kingdom

Verdant Aria is mid-sized and temperate. Three generations of agricultural surplus
made it prosperous; the political vacuum has begun to erode that. The border towns
still function well; the capital is tense. Travelers move between them freely but
notice the difference.

The nobility is divided: those who prospered under the old crown and want succession
restored (preferably to someone they can influence), those who have adapted to the
vacuum and prefer the current ambiguity, and a small faction who believe the crown
itself was a symbol of something that should not return.

## The Bardic Tradition

Bards in Verdant Aria occupy a peculiar position: they are entertainers, historians,
and rumor-carriers simultaneously. Songs in the kingdom's tradition often encode
real events in symbolic language — this was a survival mechanism during past
political instability, and the habit persists. A well-traveled bard knows three
versions of any important event: the official one, the popular one, and the one
that rhymes with uncomfortable specificity.

The song "Crown Below the Grain" is the one nobody sings in the capital anymore.
It is old enough to predate the disappearance, which means its last verse — the
one that names a hiding place — was either prophetic, planted, or something worse.

## The Gilded Eaves

The Gilded Eaves is a prosperous inn one day's ride from the capital, on the road
that once carried the court's seasonal movements. It still sees quality travelers:
minor nobles, careful merchants, the occasional envoy who insists on no written
record of their route. Borvyn, who owns and operates it, has seen enough such guests
to know that the best service is a short memory and a full glass.

## Open Threads

- What did the crown actually look like? The descriptions in songs vary in ways that
  suggest either multiple objects or a single object with contested meaning.
- Who is the hooded patron in the Gilded Eaves common room? Borvyn says he paid in
  advance and asked for no introductions.
- The eastern monastery: does it exist? Three travelers in the past decade claimed to
  have reached it. Only two came back.
